Current Chemical Engineering Program Educational Objectives (in revision)

Given below are the 2020-2026 Program Educational Objectives which are used in the Criterion 3 Student Outcomes and Criterion 4 Continuous Improvement sections of this report. The Chemical Engineering Mission Statement and Program Educational Objectives are in the process of being revised to align the CHE PEO's with the new College of Engineering mission that was published February 11, 2026.

PEO Category Description
1 Depth In engineering practice, advanced study or original research, UIC Chemical Engineering bachelor's degree graduates will be effective in applying fundamental principles, scientific knowledge, rigorous analysis, creative design, and conceptual innovation.
2 Breadth Based upon mastery of engineering in a broad, societal context, UIC Chemical Engineering graduates will have successful careers in the public or private sectors, or in the pursuit of graduate education.
3 Professionalism and Service In the service of industry, government, the engineering profession and society at large, graduates will function effectively in the complex modern work environment with clear communication, responsible teamwork, and high standards of ethics, professionalism, safety and protection of the environment.
4 Life-Long Learning and Leadership Based upon a rigorous undergraduate program that is innovative, challenging, open and supportive, graduates will enhance their skills and knowledge through life-long learning and demonstrate professional leadership.

The Program Educational Objectives are prominently listed in the Undergraduate-ChemE Major link on the home page of the departmental website: www.che.uic.edu, or https://che.uic.edu/undergraduate-programs/che-major/. Additionally, the PEOs are included in undergraduate course syllabi.

NEW Chemical Engineering Program Educational Objectives (Work in Progress)

Work in Progress: The Chemical Engineering Mission Statment and Program Educational Objectives listed below are the result of discussions at faculty meetings and faculty email correspondence in March and April 2026. The Chemical Engineering department process to revise the mission statement began in March 2026 after the College of Engineering updated its mission statement. The process aimed to identify the department’s key strengths, differentiators, and future opportunities, while aligning its culture, mission, vision, and values. The mission statement was first discussed at the March faculty meeting, and several revisions were proposed. An online survey was then sent out to the faculty to obtain additional comments, which resulted in further discussions and revisions. At the April faculty meeting, the faculty approved the mission statement to the following statement:

“The mission of the Department of Chemical Engineering is to deliver an outstanding and accessible education to a diverse student body. We prepare skilled engineers to advance both fundamental and applied research, drive innovation, and lead the development of transformative technologies that enhance economic mobility and contribute to local and global prosperity”.

In conjunction with the mission statement, the Chemical Engineering department began revising the Program Educational Objectives to align with the new College of Engineering and Chemical Engineering mission statements that were revised in March and April 2026. Given below are the new Program Educational Objectives that have been proposed by the chemical engineering faculty. The department has also queried the External Advisory Board and chemical engineering alumni on the new PEO's, and have received comments from these stakeholders. The department is in the process of obtaining feeback from the chemical engineering students with an online survey. The Director of Undergraduate Studies will also visit a sophomore, junior and senior level ChE course to explain the new PEO's in person to the students to help increase participation in the surveh. After receiving student feedback this semester, the department will update the changes to the PEO's listed below, and align our Student Learning Outcomes with the new PEO's. Further updates and feedback from our stakeholders will be presented at the ABET site visit.

PEO Category Description
1 Professional Impact Apply fundamental principles, scientific and engineering knowledge, problem-solving, critical thinking, and creative design in professional engineering practice and non-engineering fields such as law, medicine, education and business.
2 Professional Practice Contribute to industry, academia, government and entrepreneurial ventures and develop innovative solutions that address societal and technical challenges locally and globally, contributing to economic growth and upward mobility.
3 Service and Leadership Practice engineering with clear communication, leadership, teamwork, and high standards of integrity, ethical responsibility, safety and protection of the environment, thereby creating a collaborative, diverse and inclusive culture in the workplace.
4 Professional Development Reinforce skills and knowledge through graduate education, professional development courses, self-directed research, and on-the-job training and experience.
Program Educational Objectives Review Process

Periodic review and updating of the PEOs involves all constituencies of the department. The PEOs are discussed by faculty periodically at faculty meetings, with industrial constituents at EAB meetings, Engineering EXPO and ChE Industry Day, with alumni at Engineering Expo, ChE Industry Day, and an online survey, and with students by the Senior Exit Survey and review of SLOs in the ChE courses.

Input from Engineering College administration is obtained from the Office of the Associate Dean for Undergraduate Affairs at regular meetings of the COE Educational Policy Committee (EPC). This provides an avenue for maintaining consistency between departmental PEOs and the UIC and COE mission statements.

The most recent EAB meeting took place on August 14, 2020 and the most recent faculty meeting where the PEOs were reviewed took place on August 3, 2020. The PEOs were considered again but no further changes were deemed necessary. The most recent review of the alumni was through an online survey, with 40 alumni participating. Years from graduation ranged from 1 to 13 years. All PEOs received an average score of greater than 4 using a scale of 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest).

Student Learning Objectives (SLOs) that result from the PEO's are also reviewed in relation to each course by the instructors that have taught the course in the past few years. The last review of the SLOs was done in Summer 2019.
