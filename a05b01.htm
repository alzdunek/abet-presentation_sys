Feedback and Improvement #1:
Ongoing Course Improvement using Feedback-based Analysis

We maintain on ongoing feedback loop for continuous improvement of existing courses by the following procedure (Assessment Method #5). At the end of every semester, students complete the Course Climate Evaluations administered centrally at the university level. Within two weeks or so the professor receives by email the compiled survey results. The professor reviews this student feedback from the evaluations and reflects on how he/she intends to improve that course for the next year (or semester). The Course Climate Evaluations represent a regular and extensive consultation with our student constituency with tangibly positive effects on their courses and learning experiences. Faculty respond to their course evaluations with recommendations and actions to improve their courses, which are captured through self-study statements and the Faculty Performance Profiles done every July. An example of a Course Climate Evaluation is shown in Exhibit 4-5. Faculty evaluation questions include; instructor teaching effectiveness, overall quality of the course, effective use of technology, course assignments/exams/quiz alignment with course content, and course workload. The overall teaching effectiveness evaluations for each CHE faculty for the last five years are shown in Figure 4.1. All chemical engineering instructors have ratings higher than 3.25, with >70% of the faculty rating higher than 4.00, indicating greater than satisfactory to excellent ratings by our students for our instruction methods. A sample self-study statement for CHE 311 (Transport Phenomena I) is shown in Exhibit 4-9 (section 4.C). Additional self-study statements written by faculty members and course recommendations and actions will be available for inspection during the upcoming ABET site visit.

Feedback and Improvement #2:
Improvement of CHE 381/382 Chemical Engineering Laboratory I and II from Feedback-driven and Rubric Assessment Analysis

An improvement based on student feedback from the Course Climate Evaluations and rubric assessment analysis of SLO#1 has been made in the senior-level chemical engineering laboratory courses (CHE 381/382) that has closed the loop of assessment.

Improvement in SLO#1: Course improvement comments from the Course Climate Evaluations students suggested that the lab manuals needed updating in order to provide more information on how to properly analyze the experimental data. An analysis of the CHE 381/382 rubric assessment for SLO#1 in 2021 and 2022, shown in Table 4.1, confirmed lower than expected scores of students attaining a Satisfactory grade for performance indicator #7, which covers the experimental calculations and results (Final Lab report Results section). Consequently, starting in 2023, the instructor revised the lab manuals to provide better clarity on what analysis is required for the lab reports and also developed sample calculation sheets for several of the more complex experimental calculations in the humidification, gas absorption, heat transfer, distillation, process control, and membrane separation experiments. Subsequent rubric assessment analysis of this performance indicator from 2023 to 2026 shows that the percentage of students attaining a Satisfactory score increased, as well as the average rating score, with more than 80% of the students receiving a Satisfactory score. This is a case of assessment confirming the need for improvement from student feedback and the assessment verifying the improvement achieved by making course information changes.

Feedback and Improvement #3:
Rubric Assessment and Feedback-based Improvement of SLO #2: Engineering EXPO and Senior Design Showcase EXPO

An improvement based on a rubric assessment analysis of SLO#2 from the Engineering EXPO/Senior Design Showcase judging scores and feedback from the judges and senior design team mentors has been made that has closed the loop of assessment. As noted in Section 4.A.1 Assessment Method #3, the senior design teams participate in the annual Engineering EXPO as part of their formal course requirement in CHE 397 (Senior Design II). In Spring 2022, the chemical engineering department initiated a Senior Design Showcase, a complimentary event to the Engineering EXPO but with chemical engineering senior design teams only. The Senior Design Showcase is now held annually from 2024-2026 before the Engineering EXPO. Although the Engineering EXPO judges represent a broader spectrum of judges with different expertise, the Senior Design Showcase judges consist of UIC chemical engineering alumni and other chemical engineers from industry with detailed knowledge of chemical process design. An assessment was made of SLO#2 using the Engineering EXPO scores from 2021-2022, and the Senior Design Showcase scores from 2024-2026. A decrease in the % Satisfactory attainment results was observed from 2022 to 2024, as shown in Table 4.2. Average ratings were satisfactory but had also decreased slightly. Discussions between the CHE 397 instructor and senior design mentors concluded that the Senior Design Showcase judges were judging the technical content more rigorously since they have more specific chemical engineering expertise. Feedback from the Senior Design Showcase judges indicated several areas for improvement; more detailed evaluation of design alternatives and constraints (economical, environmental, social, ethical), further analysis of design factors such as cost, manufacturability and sustainability, and better preparation to answer technical questions. The CHE 397 instructor worked with the senior design team mentors to provide direction and guidance during the class lectures and to improve these technical areas. Subsequent rubric assessment analysis from 2025 to 2026 shows that the percentage of students attaining a Satisfactory score has increased, as well as the average rating score, with 80% of the students receiving a Satisfactory score in 2026. A meeting held in Spring 2026 after the Senior Design Showcase with the senior design judges, senior design team mentors, CHE 397 instructor, and DUGS, concluded that the senior design teams had made significant improvements in both technical and presentation quality of their projects due to better mentor guidance. Future improvements such as introducing a business framework, standardizing the process flow diagrams (PFDs) and keeping project topics open-ended were also recommended at this meeting and will be incorporated into the course next year.

Feedback and Improvement #4:
Feedback-based Improvement of CHE 499 - Professional Development Seminar Course

During the previous ABET review cycle (2015-2020), the CHE 499 Professional Development Seminar curriculum was revised to create a seminar series of industry-oriented chemical engineering speakers throughout the semester. Invited speakers, mainly chemical engineers working in industry, give talks on topics related to chemical engineering practice, professional development and job searching techniques. They come from a variety of industries such as oil/gas, pharmaceuticals, fine chemicals, food, patent law, alternative energy and semiconductors. The UIC Engineering Career Center also gives seminars on job-related topics. Student feedback from the Course Climate Surveys has been very positive about the structure of the seminar course. However, student feedback, both formally and informally, suggested inviting more women engineers as seminar speakers. The undergraduate student body in chemical engineering is approximately 40% women, so adding women speakers would be beneficial in providing more career experiences, advice and role models to our women students. Another suggestion made by the chemical engineering faculty during one of our faculty retreats was to add a speaker from academia. Although most of our undergraduate students pursue industry careers, there is a small but increasing population that are attending graduate school. An academic seminar speaker would provide another viewpoint on career choices and experiences in academia. Since 2022, 4 to 6 of the 12 seminar speakers have been women coming from a variety of industries, including non-traditional careers such as patent law. In addition, one academic-oriented speaker was added to the seminar series. The academic speakers have included PhD candidates, an engineer with a master's degree, and a chemical engineering faculty member. Student feedback has been very positive about these changes. These improvements, which started in Fall 2022, may also explain the upturn of SOs #4 and #7 scores in the Senior Exit Survey results (Method #4) shown in Table 4.3.

Feedback and Improvement #5:
Rubric Assessment and Ongoing Feedback-based Improvement to CHE 230 Course Curriculum

Two courses were added to the chemical engineering curriculum in 2022; CHE 230 Molecular Systems in Chemical Engineering, and CHE 330 Polymer Science. The courses are designed to introduce several topics that are important to traditional and emerging fields in chemical engineering; polymers, molecular engineering, macromolecular systems, and nanomaterials.

A rubric assessment was performed on CHE 230 (SLO#1) in Spring 2026, and a rubric assessment of CHE 330 in Fall 2022 (SLO#3) and Fall 2025 (SLO#1). The results of the rubric assessments are shown in Table 4.4. Attainment of a Satisfactory score was achieved for a majority of the students, indicating that students are developing reasonable confidence in using mathematical relationships once the variables and governing equations are clearly defined. However, the Satisfactory attainment for CHE 230 is slightly below the 65% threshold criteria. The self-study report from the course instructor has provided several areas for improvement to CHE 230; including additional in-class examples that explicitly connect phase diagrams, microconstituents, and phase-rule calculations, placing more emphasis on helping students distinguish between phases and microconstituents, and more clearly identifying which values from a phase diagram are needed for a specific calculation. These improvements will be incorporated into the next offering of the course, with further assessment of closing the loop.

Student feedback on overlap with CME 260: Student feedback from the Course Climate Evaluation has indicated a perceived overlap between ChE 230 and CME 260 Properties of Materials. While some overlap is expected because of foundational topics such as phase behavior, thermodynamics, molecular structure, and material properties naturally support multiple chemical engineering courses, this feedback will be used to refine the course more broadly. The director of undergraduate studies, undergraduate committee member Vivek Sharma, department head Vikas Berry and Ezinne Achinivu-Ibagere, course instructor, have had several meetings and discussion about changing the topics in CHE 230. Course content was reviewed to identify areas where coordination with related courses may reduce unnecessary repetition while preserving useful conceptual reinforcement. Future offerings of ChE 230 will more clearly emphasize its distinct role as a molecular systems course by consistently connecting topics to molecular structure, bonding, microstructure, structure-property relationships, and engineering applications. Feedback and Improvement #6:
Program-Level Improvement: Addition of 5 Concentrations to the Curriculum

The BS degree in Chemical Engineering has 6 optional concentrations: Biochemical Engineering, Energy & Environment, Entrepreneurship, Nanotechnology, Polymers & Molecular Engineering, and Process Automation. The last 5 concentrations were added in 2022 as a program-level improvement to the curriculum. Initial discussions about increasing the number of concentrations began in 2019 between the Department Head and the Director of Undergraduate Studies. They identified a need to offer more specialized studies in both traditional and emerging areas of chemical engineering, as well as in engineering-based entrepreneurship, ultimately deciding on the 5 concentration topics listed above because they are in areas where Department faculty have specific expertise both in research and in practice; molecular engineering, polymers, nanomaterials, energy, environment, process automation, and entrepreneurship. These specializations play a central role in biological, chemical/industrial, environmental and pharmaceutical processes and technologies and thus are important additions to the curriculum.

Timeline: The five proposed concentrations were discussed at the Chemical Engineering External Advisory Board meeting in June 2019 and were approved by the Board members. In addition, the results from a survey of chemical engineering alumni in Summer 2019 also indicated overwhelming support for the five proposed concentrations. Work then began with a faculty task force to develop the concentration curriculum; CHE required and elective courses, selective course options outside the CHE-rubric, and the proposal. After the proposal was approved by the Department, the College Education Policy Committee, and the Senate Educational Policy Committee (SCEP), the University Senate gave final approval in 2021. The concentrations were added to the curriculum, and students began signing up for the concentrations in 2022. The course requirement details for each concentration are outlined in section 5.A.2 and are also shown in detail on the Chemical Engineering Undergraduate website page: https://che.uic.edu/undergraduate-programs/che-major/.

The 400-level courses taken in the various concentrations are: Biochemical Engineering- CHE 422 Biochemical Engineering; Energy and Environment- CHE 451 Renewable Energy Technologies and a second ChE technical elective; Entrepreneurship- CHE 427 Entrepreneurship in Engineering; Nanotechnology- CHE 455 Nanoscale Systems in Chemical Engineering and a second ChE technical elective; Polymers and Molecular Engineering- CHE 454 Molecular and Macromolecular Engineering and a second ChE technical elective (CHE 425, CHE 453, CHE 455); Process Automation- CHE 433 Process Simulation with Aspen Plus.

Although the original discussions on adding the concentrations were not actually precipitated by results of assessment, a course rubric assessment on SLO #2 and SLO #3 has been undertaken on the following 400-level courses that are part of these concentrations, as shown in Table 4.5. The analysis shows that the Satisfactory attainment threshold of 65% and average rating threshold of >2.30 is more than sufficiently being met in the 400-level chemical engineering concentration courses. Self-study reports from the instructors of these courses have provided suggestions for further improvements. These include; (CHE 451) adding course materials and class discussion on the benefits and socio-economic challenges between conventional and alternative energy solutions; (CHE 455) deepening the level of understanding of the underlying physics in nanoscale systems and encouraging more critical thinking by asking students to analyze limitations or interpret experimental scenarios.

Feedback and Improvement #7:
Program-Level Continuous Improvements: Extracurricular Student Experiences - ChemE Connect, and AIChE Student Chapter Initiatives

Based on College of Engineering administration and student feedback, several improvements have been made at the program level to the chemical engineering extracurricular student experiences that will be described below.

Department Action Team/ChemE Connect

In 2023, the College of Engineering initiated several Department Action Teams within the engineering departments with the aim of improving the undergraduate community and student success. In the chemical engineering department, the Department Action Team consisted of a group of faculty, staff members, and students; Professors Betul Bilgin, Korosh Torabi, and Ezinne Achinivu-Ibagere, Laboratory and Computer Manager Jan Sagun, undergraduate student Emily Seriruk, and graduate student Nadia Nikolova. The team met regularly to discuss ideas on how to bring change from the bottom up, how to create sustainable change to the chemical engineering undergraduate education, and how to make meaningful changes in the department.

A Town Hall meeting of faculty, students and staff was held in Fall 2024, and a department climate survey was sent to the chemical engineering undergraduate and graduate students and faculty/staff. A focus group of 9 students was also interviewed. Results concluded that a majority of students are comfortable with the climate in the department. Suggestions for improvement included; creating more chemical engineering clubs, increasing faculty/student interaction and providing more programs for first-year and transfer students. The team incorporated this feedback into their recommendations. The first initiative that the ChE DAT initiated was an End-of-the-Year Banquet held in April of 2024. The banquet provides an opportunity to share information about the department and the resources available, and increases student/faculty interaction, particularly for first-year and transfer students.

In 2024, the ChE Department Action Team changed their name to ChemE Connect. It is a student-led Department Action Team (DAT) within the Chemical Engineering department acting as a departmental student advisory/community group to enhance the student experience. The ChemE Connect team consists of the same three faculty members and staff member, but now have two undergraduate students, Joshua Olusoji and Anna Cai and one graduate student, Zac Adensaya. The ChemE Connect mission is to foster a stronger sense of community and belonging within the department, especially for our students but also for faculty and staff. They also aim to bring students together by providing them with both professional development events and a social circle to talk about things that aren't related to chemical engineering. For instance, in collaboration with the AIChE student chapter, ChemE Connect has hosted student events like the Fall semester Welcome Back Picnic in 2024 and 2025, the Spring semester End-of-Year banquet in 2025 and 2026, and numerous professional development opportunities throughout the year. These events also provide an opportunity to help students connect and build closer relationships. Additionally, the goal of ChemE Connect is to celebrate our community, its students, and their achievements. These initiatives from ChemE Connect have received very positive feedback from our students, faculty, staff, and external partners such as the senior design mentors and external advisory board. The ChemE Connect team remains active and will continue to support and improve the climate of the chemical engineering department. Feedback and Improvement #8:
Program-Level Ongoing Improvement of Safety and Ethics Content in the Curriculum

Safety: The incorporation of safety-related material into the Chemical Engineering curriculum was initiated in the 2008-2014 ABET cycle and has continued up to the present, emphasizing the importance of safety training for students. The safety rubric is tracked and assessed using SO #4 (see Exhibit 4-6: Level 2 Analysis of Assessment Data). Specific safety tutorials from the AICHE website are now used regularly in several courses (CHE 201, 381, 382, 396 and 397) and include the following titles; "Introduction to Process Safety", "Hazard Recognition", "Dust Explosions", and "Chemical Reactivity Hazards". Safety assignments in the CHE 396 and CHE 397 Senior Design I and II courses are also used regularly. These safety-related assignments discuss the hazards associated with the specific processes that the student groups are designing and include a HAZOP analysis in the form of a report and presentation. Since 2015, two safety assignments have been incorporated into the CHE 381 and CHE 382 laboratory courses; an industrial R&D Chemical Hygiene Plan, and a Hazardous Chemical Safety Course reading assignment with a quiz to grade comprehension.

ChemE Car and ChemE Cube: As mentioned above, two teams of undergraduate students participate annually in the AIChE ChemE car and ChemE cube competitions. A major aspect of these competitions is a safety analysis of their project. The students complete an Engineering Design Package that includes a Job Safety Assessment Form, describing the major safety and chemical hazards involved with the design, a hazard analysis as well as identification of safety measures and safety controls. In addition, team members complete the AIChE Academy tutorial- ELA199 ChemE Car Safety Course as part of their safety training for the competition.

Undergraduate research. Finally, many of our undergraduates perform research with the faculty in their research laboratories through the CHE 392 Undergraduate Research course. These students are required to complete a UIC Environmental, Health and Safety Office (EHSO) Laboratory Training seminar in order to work in the labs. Faculty supervisors and graduate students also inform the students on the particular safety hazards associated with the research work in their laboratories.

Ethics: The incorporation of ethics-related material into the Chemical Engineering curriculum was initiated in the 2015-2020 ABET cycle and has continued up to the present, emphasizing the importance of ethics training for students. An assignment on ethics is regularly assigned in CHE 396. The AIChE Code of Ethics is covered in several courses (CHE 201, CHE 321 and CHE 396), where ethical situations related to engineering practice are given and discussion in class. Ethics as a rubric item is assessed in SO #4 and has shown steady scores of greater than 80% Satisfactory attainment in the senior written examination assessment (Exhibit 4-6 Level 2 SLO#4 analysis).

Feedback and Improvement #9:
Program-Level Future Improvements to the Curriculum: New Introductory Chemical Engineering Course (CHE 101)

Based on discussions and feedback from our key constituencies and our faculty, a proposal is being drafted to modify our undergraduate program and add an introductory chemical engineering course to our curriculum; CHE 101 Introduction to Chemical Engineering and Sustainability. One of the goals of the course is to introduce first-year students to the chemical engineering discipline since students currently don't take a chemical engineering course until their third semester (Sophomore year). The other goal is to introduce sustainable engineering topics as applied in the chemical engineering discipline to prepare students for future technologies. The course will provide an overview of the principles and unit operations of chemical engineering as practiced in traditional industries as well as in sustainable technologies.

Five key sustainable themes will be introduced: sustainable energy, sustainable health, sustainable food, sustainable air, and sustainable water. Students will explore challenges in these areas and the role of chemical engineering in developing sustainable products and processes. The topics covered will provide context to subsequent 200 and 300 level chemical engineering courses and the connection of chemical engineering to basic sciences and other engineering disciplines.

Since CHE 101 will be a 3-credit hour course, the program course requirements will need to be modified in order to keep the total credit hour requirement at 128 hours. Possible options that have been suggested are:

  • Remove CHEM 234 as a requirement
  • Create a Selective option between CHE 230 and CME 260
  • Remove CHE 342 Physical Chemical I as a requirement

Faculty, External Advisory Board, and Alumni input is ongoing, and a task force consisting of several faculty members (including the DUGS) has been initiated to decide on the best option.
