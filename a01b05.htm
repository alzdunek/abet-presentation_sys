Prerequisite requirements for non-Engineering courses are done by the department where the course resides, and verification that a student has the necessary pre-requisites for these courses are also done by the course’s department, not by the College of Engineering or Chemical Engineering Department. We rely on the non-Engineering course departments to monitor their courses and deny course registration if a course pre-requisite is not satisfied. Generally, the monitoring of pre-requisites for non-Engineering science and math courses has been excellent.

For Student #2, the CHEM 342 course instructor was contacted about the PHYS 142 prerequisite. The CHEM 342 course instructor replied with an email (see below) that as the instructor of record, he granted Student #2 an override for the PHYS 142 pre-requisite and allowed the student to register for the course. The chemistry department allows instructors to waive pre-requisites.

Question #1 and #11 Combined: Assessment of the Concentrations; how assessment is done across all concentrations and any differences depending on concentration

The concentrations in the chemical engineering program are assessed by several methods across the concentrations: Method #1: Grading rubrics of the ChE electives required in the concentrations, self-study course reports by instructors for the ChE electives in the concentrations, faculty retreat and EAB meeting discussions, and enrollment analysis in each concentration. The different assessment methods used assure that the concentrations are assessed directly (Method #1) and indirectly (self-study reports, faculty/EAB discussions, student course climate surveys). Future analysis and assessment are planned to determine if new ChE electives and other non-ChE courses can be added to enhance the topics covered in a specific concentration.

Direct Assessment

Course rubric assessments were performed on six, 400-level CHE courses for SLO #2 and SLO #3 as shown in the Table below. The six courses cover the concentrations: Biochemical Engineering (CHE 422), Process Automation (CHE 433), Energy and Environment (CHE 451, CHE 453), Polymer and Molecular Engineering (CHE 425, CHE 455), and Nanotechnology (CHE 425, CHE 455). The analysis shows that the Satisfactory attainment threshold of 65% and average rating threshold of >2.30 is more than sufficiently being met in the 400-level chemical engineering concentration courses.

An analysis of student grades in the 400-level courses has been undertaken from 2024 to 2026. The results show that 92-95% of the students have received either an A or B grade in the various courses, indicating an excellent level of understanding by the majority of the students taking these courses.

Indirect Assessment

Self-study reports from the instructors of these courses have provided suggestions for further improvements. These include; (CHE 451) adding course materials and class discussion on the benefits and socio-economic challenges between conventional and alternative energy solutions; (CHE 455) deepening the level of understanding of the underlying physics in nanoscale systems and encouraging more critical thinking by asking students to analyze limitations or interpret experimental scenarios. Instructors also use the UIC Course Climate Evaluations that the students complete at the end of each semester to help facilitate continuous improvement of their courses.

Future Improvments to the Concentrations

Future analysis and assessment are planned to determine if new ChE electives can be added to enhance the topics covered in a specific concentration. For example, a new 400-level course, CHE 426 Biopharmaceutical Process Development and Manufacturing has been recently added as a permanent technical elective course in the program. CHE 426 might be a good fit an elective course option in the Biochemical Engineering concentration.

An indirect assessment of the non-ChE courses in the concentrations is also planned to obtain student feedback on the non-ChE courses taken by the CHE students. A review of the non-ChE course options will also be made to determine if new, non-ChE courses can be added that may provide more depth and knowledge to the particular concentration topic. For instance, PHYS 411 Quantum Mechanics or CME 460 Crystallography and X-Ray Diffraction are possible courses that may be added to the Nanotechnology concentration to strengthen the topics.

Associate Dean Miiri Kotche asked Malinda Lorkovich, Assistant Vice Provost for Enrollment Management in the Enrollment Management Office to clarify UIC's ACT/SAT test option policy. Malinda's response was,

Question #3: Full-Time Equivalent comparison to Full-Time Head Count

The College of Engineering uses the full-time student headcount for the Student FTE row. That is why the FTE numbers are identical to the full-time student numbers in Table 1.5 in the Self-Study Report. However, Associate Dean Miiri Kotche asked Thomas Warfield, Executive Director, Office of Institutional Research for clarification of the FTE definition. Based on Thomas’ clarification that uses the U.S. Department of Education, National Center for Education Statistics, Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System (IPEDS) definition of FTE, if we want the table to reflect the IPEDS fall FTE methodology, the calculation should instead be:

Estimated FTE = Full-Time Headcount + (Part-Time Headcount × 0.403543)

Questions #4, #5 and #7 Answers Combined: New Program Educational Objective Development Narrative, Mechanism for Student Feedback, and Revision Progress
Revision Narrative

#1:Mission Statement Development The Chemical Engineering department process to revise the mission statement began in March 2026 after the College of Engineering updated its mission statement. The Chemical Engineering Mission Statement and Program Educational Objectives listed below are the result of discussions at faculty meetings and faculty email correspondence in March and April 2026. The process aimed to identify the department’s key strengths, differentiators, and future opportunities, while aligning its culture, mission, vision, and values. The mission statement was first discussed at the March faculty meeting, and several revisions were proposed. An online survey was then sent by email to the faculty to obtain additional comments, which resulted in further discussions and revisions. At the April faculty meeting, the faculty approved the mission statement to the following statement:

“The mission of the Department of Chemical Engineering is to deliver an outstanding and accessible education to a diverse student body. We prepare skilled engineers to advance both fundamental and applied research, drive innovation, and lead the development of transformative technologies that enhance economic mobility and contribute to local and global prosperity”.

#2:PEO Development The Chemical Engineering department began revising the Program Educational Objectives in April 2026 to align with the new College of Engineering and Chemical Engineering mission statements that were revised in March and April 2026. Using newly proposed ECE Department PEO's as a template obtained from the College of Engineering ABET coordinator, Lo'Ay AbuSalah, a revised set of Chemical Engineering PEO's were drafted by the CHE Director of Undergraduate Studies, reviewed by the Department Head, and then sent by email through several iterations to the CHE faculty for their review, comment, and approval in May 2026.

After the faculty approved the first draft, an online survey was sent by email to the External Advisory Board and chemical engineering alumni to provide feedback on the new PEO's. Comments from the surveys resulted in further revisions to the PEO's.

Student Feedback Mechanism for new PEO's

In September 2026, The Director of Undergraduate Studies visited a sophomore (CHE 210), junior (CHE 311) and senior level (CHE 499) to explain the new PEO's in person to the students and obtain comments. A QR code for an online survey was also provided to the students, and an email with the QR code was sent to all chemical engineering students for further feedback and acknowledgement.

Given below are the latest Program Educational Objectives that have been reviewed by the chemical engineering faculty, EAB members, alumni, and students.

Question #6: ChemE Connect Description

ChemE Connect is a student-led Department Action Team (DAT) within the Chemical Engineering department acting as a departmental student advisory/community group to enhance student experience.

The ChemE Connect Mission Statement is:

  • To foster a stronger sense of community and belonging within the department, especially for our students, but also for faculty and staff.
  • To bring students together by providing them with both professional development events and a social circle to talk about things that aren't related to chemical engineering.

ChemE Connect is designed to foster community, student engagement, and volunteerism among chemical engineering majors:

Student Involvement: The program relies on undergraduate volunteers to host events, support peer networking, and bridge connections within the department.

Networking & Support: ChemE Connect runs alongside other vital department resources like the AICHE UIC Student Chapter and the Engineering Career Center to build a community inside the Engineering Innovation Building.

Events: In collaboration with the AIChE student chapter, ChemE Connect has hosted student events like the Fall semester Welcome Back Picnic, the Spring semester End-of-Year banquet, and numerous professional development opportunities throughout the year. These events also provide an opportunity to help students connect and build closer relationships.

Additionally, the goal of ChemE Connect is to celebrate our community, its students, and their achievements. The ChemE Connect team remains active and will continue to support and improve the climate of the chemical engineering department.

Further details on the background and history of ChemE Connect as well as specific examples of ChemE Connect events and student surveys are given in section 4. Continuous Improvement -Feedback and Improvement #7 of this presentation system.
