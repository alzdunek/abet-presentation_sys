Institutional Support for the Chemical Engineering Program at the College and University Levels

Undergraduate Academic Advising

UIC Engineering's Academic Resource Center provides academic advising and support for engineering undergraduate students. There are 5 advisors assigned to all the engineering students based the first letter of the student’s last name.

Engineering Career Center

The Engineering Career Center (ECC) provides helpful resources specifically geared toward UIC College of Engineering students and alumni for successfully securing internships, co-ops and jobs. ECC provides virtual and in-person career advising via scheduled appointments and drop-in advising. The University provides a free job platform for both students and employers.

Students can schedule an appointment for an in-person one-on-one career advising meeting, they can also attend small group sessions for resume reviews, elevator pitch and interviewing skills. Students attend two specific engineering and computer science career fairs per academic year put on by the ECC; students also receive marketing information on other career fairs conducted by campus-wide career offices. ECC conducts presentations to Chemical Engineering classes including the engineering orientation course titled Engineering 100, and the Chemical Engineering Professional Development Seminar CHE 499. The Chemical Engineering department and the ECC have had a close working relationship since 2016.

The ECC is also responsible for implementing the Early Access to Paid Internships Program (EAPIP) for new freshmen and new transfer students. The goal of the program is to help students gain internship experience early in their college career. Students who meet the requirements are granted access to an exclusive internship database, where faculty and other supervisors recruit for on-campus internships. Students apply with a resume and interview for these internships.

Equity & Inclusion in Engineering Program (formerly MEERP)

The Equity & Inclusion in Engineering Program (EIEP), formerly called the Minority Engineering Recruitment and Retention Program (MERRP), is dedicated to empowering students in the College of Engineering to pursue and thrive in a UIC engineering education. The mission of EIEP is to empower historically underrepresented populations, first-generation students, members of the 2SLGBTQIA+ community, and all students in the College of Engineering to thrive in engineering and technology. EIEP fosters a welcoming environment that champions persistence and excellence at UIC Engineering and beyond through comprehensive support, including academic advising, scholarships, mentorship, and inclusive programming. The program is committed to supporting students’ persistence in engineering by providing tailored guidance on academic pathways, personal growth, and professional development. The EIEP staff is composed of professionals with backgrounds in higher education, engineering, and engineering education. EIEP has supported the student chapters of the Society of Hispanic Professional Engineers (SHPE) and the National Society of Black Engineers (NSBE) and has been a source of experience and advice for both groups. The hallmark of EIEP is Supplemental Instruction (SI) and the “academic family” atmosphere.

Women in Engineering Program (WIE)

The goal of the UIC Women in Engineering Program, WIE, is to increase the number of women students pursuing and graduating in science, technology, engineering and math, STEM, disciplines, and to promote the recruitment, retention and advancement of women who have chosen academic careers. WIE supports women undergrad/graduate students and faculty in STEM by sponsoring activities that foster a positive educational and professional environment, and enable excellence in scholarship, teaching and service. Since 2016, the chemical engineering department has maintained 35 to 40% female enrollment in our undergraduate program. Many of the Chemical Engineering women students participate in WIE activities and are active members in the Society of Women Engineering (SWE).

Program Budget and Financial Support

The institutional budget for the Department of Chemical Engineering consists of two parts, personnel and the operating costs. The personnel costs are those for salaries of faculty, union staff members, academic professionals and teaching assistants. The budgeted amount for these salaries increases each year by an average percentage mandated by the University. These increases have so far been to retain qualified personnel in the department. The budget of the College is currently in part tied to the enrollment in Engineering and the tuition revenue generated by the enrolled students. While the Department budget is not tied directly to enrollments, it is heavily influenced by it. TThe department has had a decrease in student enrollments in the last few years that has followed the national trend in chemical engineering department enrollments, partially due to the pandemic. However, in 2025 and in 2026 the department has seen a steady increase in student enrollments. The budget for the operating costs has not increased for the last few years due to statewide economic conditions and has in fact decreased slightly in the last fiscal year. The department has three accounts where it generates additional revenue: (a) The Indirect Cost account (the fraction of the overhead funds available from research grants that is returned to the department), (b) Gift account (gift funds given by benefactors of the department) and (c) Educational Program account (tuition revenue from other educations programs). These additional funds have therefore also been used to ensure that the program objectives are met. Some examples of how these funds have been used in meeting program objectives are given below:

  1. Updating Departmental Computing Services: The funds have been used to purchase site licenses for software needed by our students and faculty for their classroom activities, such as ASPEN Plus, and CHEMCAD.

  2. New Unit Operations Laboratory Equipment: Digital balance, digital hygrometer, lab computers, portable air compressor.

  3. Professional Development Supplement: The faculty receive a professional development funds from the university (~$900 for non-tenure-track and $1200 for tenure-track). The above mentioned funds have been used to supplement the funds from university for teaching workshops, and conference visits.

  4. Startup Packages: The department hired one assistant professor in 2022 and one associate professor in 2020. The departmental share of the startup funds is covered by these funds.

The current sources of financial support for the Department are the State of Illinois with additional revenue mentioned above. The state funding covers faculty and staff salary, as well as some additional non-permanent positions such as Teaching Assistants and part-time student workers. This portion of the budget is fixed each year by the University -- changes made are generally consistent with the support provided to the University by the State of Illinois. In addition the Department receives a portion of the Indirect Cost Recovery (ICR) from research grants housed in the department, or those in which the department faculty participate. Currently the University returns 47.5% of the overhead cost to the College of Engineering, and this is then divided equally between the College and the Department. The Department actively works with the Development Office of the College and University to raise gifts from alumni and local and national businesses with an interest in our Department and University. These gifts when they are discretionary are also used to fund special projects in the department including sometimes emergency repairs to equipment in the UG Laboratories that has not been budgeted. Our development activities have resulted in one major gift and several smaller gifts. The major gift is being used for an endowed professorship position in the department

While there has been no increase in state budget over a period of time, the alternate revenue streams have seen an increase driven by an increase in our student enrollments and our international programs. This together with careful budgetary management in general by the Dean’s office has allowed us to providing a high quality education for the undergraduate students.

In 2019, the department moved into our new $45M building (Engineering Innovation Building) that houses the entire department. The building has a state-of-the-art classroom with a large touchscreen. Further, the room is annexed to our unit-operations laboratory via a large window through which students in the lecture room can see the various equipment; a model that was inspired by the medical observatory classrooms. The building also has student learning space spread throughout the building, including a computational room, library, lounge, and atrium space for events. College alumni donor funding for the scholarships and computer software is another example of support for our program. Strong support enables effectiveness in teaching and learning. A measure of the quality of instruction we have provided for our students is the Annual College of Engineering EXPO. In the past years, our students have won a higher share of awards. The department has also initiated a Chemical Engineering Design Showcase with chemical engineering specific judges that complements the College of Engineering EXPO.
