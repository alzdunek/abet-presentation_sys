Engineering and Innovation Building

The chemical engineering department moved into its new home in the Engineering Innovation Building (EIB) in summer 2019. The 57,500-square-foot-building is at the corner of Morgan and Taylor Streets on UIC’s east campus and is shared by the Chemical Engineering, Civil and Materials, and Mechanical Engineering Departments; the Chemical Engineering department is the largest tenant. The EIB is a state-of-the-art facility designed by Dewberry architects and built by Berglund Construction in 2018-2019 and was LEED Gold certified in 2021. The new building features high-tech classrooms and laboratories, including vibration-dampened nanotechnology labs, biomedical research rooms, molecular engineering research labs, and a highly attuned darkroom for complex fluid imaging.

Faculty Offices:

    The Engineering Innovation Building contains 16 offices for chemical engineering faculty located on the first and second floors, two offices for teaching assistants (EIB 264 and 276), and one central office for the administrative staff (EIB 226). Faculty offices have a 43” LED 4KTV monitor mounted on the wall for student discussions. There is secure WIFI connectivity throughout the building.

Classrooms:

    There are two classrooms in EIB, Rooms 124 and 271. Both rooms are equipped with moveable tablet armchairs, high-definition video projectors and an Integrated Multimedia lectern with an installed computer, laptop connectivity, a document camera, a Blu-Ray player and internet connectivity. Room 124 has a state-of-the-art, lecture board containing a MultiTouch Video Wall System of eight, 55" LCD video wall displays, and a moveable whiteboard. Room 271 has a 75” LED 4KTV monitor for lectures as well as a whiteboard. Most chemical engineering courses are taught in these two classrooms, but other university classrooms with similar features are also utilized in order to provide the best class schedule for the students.

Laboratories:
  • Unit Operations Laboratory

    The Unit Operations Laboratory for CHE 381 and 382 is located on the first floor of EIB in Room 128. A unique feature is that can be viewed from classroom EIB 124 by a large viewing window. The unit operations laboratory contains 12 functional chemical engineering process demonstrators supporting hands-on instruction of chemical engineering principles such as heat, mass and momentum transfer, fluid properties, and separations processes such as distillation, evaporation, absorption, filtration and extraction. The laboratory space is specifically designed as an instructional chemical engineering laboratory with dedicated workspaces for the process equipment and easy access to utilities such as 120V and 220V electricity, industrial hot and chilled water, purified water, compressed air, and vacuum lines. Eight moveable lab stations and two permanent lab bench areas are located throughout the lab and for the analytical equipment used in the process experiments. A 48kW electric steam generator was installed in December 2019 and is plumbed directly to the appropriate process equipment. There are two fume hoods and four dropdown snorkel lines for chemical vapor control. The laboratory is furnished with two (2) computers connected to the distillation column and process control demonstrators, respectively. High-speed WiFi and ethernet connections to the UIC network are both available in the lab. Most students use personal laptop computers, tablets, or other devices.

  • ChemE Car/ChemE Cube Laboratory

    The EIB 231 laboratory on the second floor is used by the two student teams that participate in the AIChE ChemE Car and ChemE Cube annual competitions. The laboratory has 6 moveable lab stations, one fume hood, and compressed air lines, as well as access to 120V and 220V electricity. The ChemE Car and ChemE Cube teams use this laboratory to develop and test their prototype models.

  • Engineering Makerspace

    The University of Illinois Chicago (UIC) Engineering Makerspace is a premier, dedicated fabrication and prototyping facility that supports the academic and professional development of engineering students, faculty, and student organizations within the College of Engineering (CoE). Located in the Roosevelt Road Building on the UIC campus, the Makerspace comprises over 25,000 square feet of program space equipped with more than 100 machines and hundreds of hand tools, facilitating a wide range of design, fabrication, and testing activities fundamental to modern engineering education and innovation. Makerspace offers three courses: ENGR 111 Additive Manufacturing, ENGR 112 Subtractive Machining and ENGR 214 Advanced Manufacturing Methods.

    A broad array of fabrication technologies is available within Makerspace. The Digital Fabrication Lab houses multiple 3D printers (e.g., Voron Trident, Stratasys Objet30 Prime, Formlabs Form3+), CNC mills, laser cutters, and waterjet systems. Supporting technologies include textiles and vinyl cutters, electronics assembly and testing stations, 2D/3D scanning and photography equipment, and silicone molding and resin casting tools. Traditional Machine Shop capabilities include vertical mills, lathes, carpentry tools, and manual and CNC equipment. The facility also includes collaborative workshop areas and common project spaces that encourage teamwork and knowledge sharing.

Computing Resources

The Chemical Engineering department provides a special license (150) and installation of Aspen V14 Process Simulation software to the students that can be installed on the student laptops. The department also has 22 rental laptops running Windows 11 Enterprise for the students in case they are in need of a PC-Windows computer. Aspen is specific to Windows operating system. The university also provides complimentary installation of Microsoft Office 365, MATLAB, and Adobe products to the students, faculty, and staff. The Chemical Engineering department also has a B/W laser printer for student used in EIB 276, a main office printer in the department office (EIB 224) for b/w and color printing jobs, and a 36-inch poster printer for student poster presentations for conferences and the senior design EXPO.

In the Unit Operations Laboratory, there are 2 computers that are dedicated to running the distillation column and process control demonstrators. High-speed WIFI and ethernet connections to the UIC network are both available in the lab. Most students use personal laptop computers, tablets, or other devices.

Aside from computers dedicated to student use in the Chemical Engineering Building (CEB), computer labs are distributed throughout the campus – although the vast majority are concentrated on the east campus within easy walking distance of EIB.

All these computers are installed with Excel and MATLAB and Microsoft Office 365, which are the main software packages that Chemical Engineering students would need.

For advanced computing needs and high-performance computing (HPC) on campus, the students have access to two clusters, Extreme and SABER. Extreme is a powerful machine with 3,500 cores, 24.5 Terabytes of memory and 1.25 Petabytes of local raw storage, of which 275 Terabytes is raw fast scratch storage. The cluster is composed of traditional, high memory and GPU computing nodes: 200 HPC worker nodes, 160 x Generation I: Intel Xeon E5-2670 @ 2.60 GHz, cache size of 20MB with 16 cores in each node, 128GB RAM and 1TB local storage; 40 x Generation II: Intel Xeon E5-2670 @2.50 GHz, cache size of 20MB with 20 cores per node, 128 GB RAM and 1TB local storage; 3 x Generation I High Memory Worker Nodes: Intel Xeon E5-4650L @ 2.60GHz, cache size of 20MB with 32 cores per node with 1TB RAM; 50 x Generation II GPU Worker Nodes; 3 x Login Nodes; and 2 x Admin Nodes. The traditional HPC and high memory worker nodes currently run CentOS 6.9. The GPU worker nodes currently run CentOS 7.5.

The Shared Analytics & Big-data Enterprise Resource (SABER) was introduced into the Advanced Cyberinfrastructure for Education and Research (ACER) HPC resource offerings in May 2017. In addition to traditional HPC workloads, this NSF-funded cluster provides access to various Big-Data/Analytics software packages. The SABER cluster consists of traditional high-performance computing nodes; 76 x HPC Nodes with Intel Xeon E5-2650 @ 2.20GHz; 24 x Cores/Node with 30 MB cache, 128GB RAM, 1TB Local Storage. Software on each node is CentOS 7.3.

University Facilities

UIC Innovation Center

The University of Illinois Chicago (UIC) Innovation Center is a cross-disciplinary hub that connects students, faculty, and industry partners to advance experiential learning, interdisciplinary collaboration, and applied innovation. The Innovation Center’s mission is to drive innovation forward through hands-on engagement and partnerships that ignite student success, deepen academic–industry ties, and generate real-world solutions to complex challenges. Serving as a central bridge between academia and industry, the Innovation Center engages participants from more than 28 disciplines across the university community. It has collaborated with over 38 unique corporate partners and trained more than 1,200 students in collaborative solution development.

At the heart of the Innovation Center’s activities are labs, classes, and workshops that offer structured, experiential opportunities for students and faculty to work on real-world problems. These activities are designed to foster a human-centered approach to innovation, emphasizing empathy, creative problem solving, iterative testing, and teamwork. Labs operate as ongoing collaborative environments where interdisciplinary teams engage in sustained innovation work. Examples include Medical Device Labs and User Experience Labs, where students and industry partners co-develop solutions that address healthcare, usability, and systems challenges. These labs allow participants to apply classroom knowledge to tangible projects, encouraging technical proficiency, design thinking, and professional readiness.

Classes and programmatic offerings at the Innovation Center reinforce critical and creative thinking through project-based learning. Courses such as Interdisciplinary Solution Development (IXD) and Entrepreneurial Solution Development (EXD) bring together students from diverse majors to solve industry-sponsored problems or to identify and pursue unmet needs independently. These opportunities help students deepen analytical skills, build professional competencies, and navigate complex, multi-stakeholder environments.

    • Guidance

    Undergraduates performing research in faculty laboratories are required to attend a UIC Environmental Health and Safety Office (EHSO) laboratory safety training course before working in the laboratory. Information on the safety courses available can be found at:

    https://ehso.uic.edu/training/introduction-to-laboratory-safety-training/

    Undergraduates enrolled in the unit operations laboratory (CHE 381 and 382) are provided training and guidance by the instructor and teaching assistants on proper usage and safety precautions before they begin their experimental work on the chemical process demonstrators. This is initially done in the first lab period of the semester, and thereafter during ‘pre-lab’ laboratory periods. Additional guidance and oversight is provided while the lab teams are performing the experiments and afterwards to ensure proper equipment shut-off procedures.

    Makerspace staff provide guidance to students before they can use the machining tools and equipment. Three courses for more detailed instruction are also offered; ENGR 111 Additive Manufacturing, ENGR 112 Subtractive Machining and ENGR 214 Advanced Manufacturing Methods.
